Deep Learning Indaba 2026 — RIAD Poster

Tone on a Budget: A Reference-Free Metric for Lexical Tone in Massively-Multilingual TTS

Daudu, M., Felix, F.†, & Bezaleel, H.-J.† — ML Collective (†equal contribution) (2026)

Introduces tone_i2, the first automated, reference-free tone-error metric for synthesised African speech. CER is structurally blind to lexical tone (Yorùbá ọkọ́ vs. ọkọ̀ are identical to CER); tone_i2 catches degradations CER misses, oracle-validated via pitch-flattening and answer-key-flip controls (AUROC 0.96 vs. native-speaker judgements). Applied to OmniVoice (646-language TTS): Yorùbá tone is already near-native zero-shot, while a few hours of fine-tuning buys intelligibility (CER 5.6%→1.8%), not tone — a distinction CER alone cannot make. Roadmap extends the benchmark to six African languages (Yorùbá ✓, Swahili ✓, Hausa, Igbo, isiZulu, Nigerian English).

WiML @ ICML 2026 — Poster

Why Large Language Models Fail for Hausa Educational Content: Cascading Errors From Translation to Speech to Comprehension

Bezaleel, H.-J., Jim, P., & Daudu, M. — ML Collective, UniCBT (2025)

Evaluates end-to-end LLM pipelines on HausaPQ-Speech — a new dataset of 4,703 WASSCE exam questions. MT exhibits 6.9–13.9% omission rates; ASR achieves 68–92% WER with 100% tonal vowel error rate across all models. LLM cascading for error correction yields minimal improvement (<15% WER) or causes hallucination. QA accuracy remains below 30%, with Hausa-specialised models underperforming general multilingual models. Concludes that fully automated systems are not viable for Hausa educational deployment; human-in-the-loop approaches remain essential.

HausaVoicePQ: A Validated Multimodal Dataset for Hausa Educational Question Answering.

Bezaleel, H.-J.

Proposes a multimodal hausa educational dataset Designed and implemented a four-stage cascaded NLP pipeline: fine-tuned NLLB-200-distilled-600M for English→Hausa machine translation (trained on a single Tesla GPU under real compute constraints), a fine-tuned VITS model for Hausa TTS synthesis, Whisper-large-hausa for ASR, and Llama-3.3-70B for downstream transcript error correction); built automated data pipelines for dataset creation, preprocessing, and bilingual text–audio alignment. Developed evaluation and benchmarking workflows using WER, CER, and MOS, validating performance on the HausaPQ-Speech corpus (1,000 human-validated samples); identified critical performance gaps, including WER variance (68–92%) and 100% tonal error rate in baseline ASR models (Whisper, Wav2Vec2). Conducted systematic failure-mode analysis across the pipeline, uncovering key issues such as semantic omission in MT, lexical diversity collapse (TTR: 17.3% → 13.9%), LLM hallucination, and reasoning degradation (82% → <30% accuracy) in downstream tasks. Optimised pipeline performance for real-world multilingual deployment, replacing closed-source MT with fine-tuned NLLB-200 for reproducibility and implementing structural normalisation and inference stabilisation techniques in compute-constrained environments.
